# W. Ross Ashby and requisite variety

W. Ross Ashby's An Introduction to Cybernetics (1956) defined variety as the number of distinguishable states of a system, or the logarithm of that number to base two. His Law of Requisite Variety says that only variety can destroy variety: a regulator can hold the outcomes of a system within bounds only if it commands at least as much variety as the disturbances it must counter. The variety of outcomes can be no smaller than the variety of the disturbances less the variety of the regulator.

Stafford Beer later restated the law for management as "variety absorbs variety", and built his practice of variety engineering on it: attenuate the variety that reaches a regulator, amplify the variety of its response, until the two balance.

Ashby's earlier book, Design for a Brain (1952), and his Homeostat, a machine that found its own stable configuration under disturbance, were among the founding works of cybernetics.
