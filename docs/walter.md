# W. Grey Walter and the tortoises

William Grey Walter, a neurophysiologist at the Burden Neurological Institute in Bristol, built two small autonomous robots between 1948 and 1949. He called the species Machina speculatrix, the machine that looks, and the two individuals Elmer and Elsie. Each had a photocell that scanned the room, a shell that closed a contact when it touched anything, a drive motor, a steering motor, and a "brain" of two valves.

From that reflex the tortoises sought moderate light, turned away from bright light, butted their way around obstacles, danced in front of a mirror when they caught sight of their own pilot lamp, and went home to a lit hutch to recharge when their batteries ran low. Walter demonstrated them by the end of 1949, described them in Scientific American in 1950 as "An Imitation of Life", and showed later machines at the Festival of Britain in 1951.

His point was about brains: that life-like behaviour needs very few components if they are wired into a tight feedback loop with the world.
