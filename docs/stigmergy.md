# Stigmergy and termite-inspired construction

Stigmergy is coordination through the environment: an agent's action changes the shared surroundings, and that change, not a message, is what prompts the next action. The term comes from the entomologist Pierre-Paul Grassé's work on termite nest-building in the 1950s.

In 2014 Justin Werfel, Kirstin Petersen and Radhika Nagpal published "Designing Collective Behavior in a Termite-Inspired Robot Construction Team" in Science. Their TERMES robots built towers, castles and pyramids out of foam bricks using only local sensing, with no supervisor and no communication between robots; each followed fixed local rules and read the state of the growing structure to decide what to do next. A target structure was specified offline and compiled into those local rules, so what the system removed was the runtime foreman, not the plan.

The architects R&Sie(n) had earlier imagined, in the speculative project "I've heard about…" shown at the Musée d'Art Moderne de la Ville de Paris in 2005, a city that grows by local accretion under structural constraints they called viability.
