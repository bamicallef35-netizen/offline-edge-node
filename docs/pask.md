# Gordon Pask, Musicolour and the Colloquy of Mobiles

Gordon Pask's Musicolour, developed from about 1953 with Robin McKinnon-Wood at System Research Ltd, was a light show driven by a live musician. Its response thresholds rose whenever a musical figure was repeated, so the machine grew bored of repetition and went dark until the performer tried something new. It rewarded novelty.

His Colloquy of Mobiles was shown at the exhibition Cybernetic Serendipity at the ICA in London in 1968: five computer-controlled hanging mobiles with internal drives that sought a transient satisfaction, exchanging light and sound. Visitors joined in with mirrors and torches and became part of the conversation.

Pask called such systems aesthetically potent environments. In 1969 he published "The Architectural Relevance of Cybernetics" in Architectural Design, arguing that a building could be in conversation with its occupants. Earlier in the decade he had been cybernetician to Cedric Price and Joan Littlewood's unbuilt Fun Palace.
