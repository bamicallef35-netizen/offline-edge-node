# Stafford Beer and the Viable System Model

Stafford Beer set out the Viable System Model in Brain of the Firm (1972), developed it in The Heart of Enterprise (1979), and gave it a practitioner's form in Diagnosing the System for Organizations (1985). The model holds that any organization able to maintain itself has five necessary functions: operational units that do the work (System 1), coordination between them (System 2), internal regulation with a sporadic audit channel (Systems 3 and 3*), intelligence about the environment and the future (System 4), and policy or identity (System 5). Each operational unit is itself a viable system; Beer called this recursion.

Alarm signals that bypass the normal hierarchy he called algedonic, from the Greek for pain and pleasure.

Beer tried the model on a national scale in Project Cybersyn in Chile between 1971 and 1973, with Chilean engineers including Fernando Flores and Raúl Espejo, linking factories to an operations room in Santiago by telex. The project was unfinished when the coup of 11 September 1973 ended it. Eden Medina's Cybernetic Revolutionaries (2011) is the standard history.
